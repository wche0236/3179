Outdoor Recreation in Australia

Recreation shapes everyday life across Australia. This dashboard explores participation, changing habits, public spaces, national parks and barriers.

01

Participation and people

Who Participates, and How?

Start with the scale of participation, then compare how involvement differs across activities and life stages.

Key finding

Walking reaches 11.3 million adults.

Walking is the most widely participated-in activity in this comparison,
reaching approximately 11.3 million adults.
Bush walking follows, reaching 4.0 million.
The gap shows how strongly participation is concentrated in an accessible,
everyday activity compared with more specialised pursuits.

Key finding

Golf has the largest male share.

Men represent 81% of golf participants and
78% of fishing participants. Women represent
58% of walking participants. These differences show that participation is
not uniform across activities.
They describe who takes part, rather than explaining why these patterns occur.

Key finding

Older adults increasingly favour non-sport activities.

Among active teenagers, 60% participate in both.
Another 26% participate in sport only.

Among active adults aged sixty-five and above,
64% participate in non-sport activities only.
Just 8% participate in sport only.

Activity preferences change across different life stages.
Accessible options remain important for older communities.

Key finding

Weekly participation varies between Australian states.

Participation rates range from 67% to 78%.
The Australian Capital Territory records the highest rate.

State differences may reflect varied local circumstances.
National averages can conceal these regional differences.

02

Change over time

How Have Recreation Habits Changed?

Explore five activities across several survey years. Compare changing participation patterns over time.

Long-term change

Bush walking grew rapidly before recent survey changes.

Bush walking increased by 75% between 2016–17 and 2022–23.
Walking increased by approximately 9%.

Recent trends

Cycling has declined since its 2021 peak.

Cycling peaked at 3.2 million participants in 2020–21.
Participation declined after that peak.

03

Places and public spaces

Where Does Recreation Happen?

Explore recreation settings and national park locations. Compare park distribution with reported visitation.

Key finding

Public spaces support a substantial share of activity.

Public spaces account for 31 out of 100 reported settings.
They exceed clubs, gyms and homes individually.

Shared outdoor spaces support everyday recreation.
Their accessibility matters beyond national parks.

Key finding

Recorded national parks concentrate along coastal regions.

Queensland records 206 parks.
New South Wales records 169.
Together, they represent approximately 63% of 599 parks.

Key finding

Three Sydney-region parks dominate recorded visitation.

Blue Mountains received 7.8 million domestic visits.
Royal received 4.9 million.
Ku-ring-gai Chase received 4.3 million.

Together, these parks represent approximately 62%
of visits across the ten parks displayed.

04

Motivations and access

What Encourages or Prevents Participation?

Explore motivations, barriers and spending differences. Consider how experiences vary across demographic groups.

Key finding

Motivations shift from enjoyment towards health.

Fun and enjoyment lead among younger participants.
Physical health becomes more prominent among older adults.

Outdoor enjoyment remains relevant across age groups.
Recreation messaging can reflect these changing priorities.

Key finding

Reported barriers differ between men and women.

Women report affordability barriers at 8.4%.
Men report the same barrier at 4.5%.

Disliking physical activity affects 16.8% of women.
The figure for men is 11.5%.

Men more frequently report having physically demanding jobs.
Different barriers may require different support strategies.

Key finding

Recreation spending follows different age patterns.

Women's average spending peaks around $1,760 at ages forty-five to fifty-four.
It falls to approximately $1,055 among those aged sixty-five and above.

Men's spending peaks around $1,790 at ages fifty-five to sixty-four.
Average spending remains approximately $1,715 among older men.

Spending describes the cost of participation.
It does not establish whether spending causes participation.

The bigger picture

What This Means

  1. Recreation often begins with everyday activities.

    Walking reaches 11.4 million adults. Non-sport activities become increasingly important with age.

  2. Accessible public spaces support active communities.

    Public spaces represent the most common reported setting. National park visitation also concentrates near Sydney.

  3. Participation needs differ across communities.

    Motivations change with age. Affordability and enjoyment also influence reported barriers.

Protect accessible recreation spaces across Australian communities. Tailor support to different needs and life stages.
