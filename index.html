AUSTRALIAN RECREATION INSIGHTS

Outdoor Recreation in Australia

Explore how Australians engage with outdoor recreation, from popular activities and participation differences to motivations, barriers and national parks.

01

How Do Australians Participate?

Discover popular activities and differences between male and female participants.

Recreational walking attracts far more participants than specialised activities, suggesting that accessible, everyday forms of recreation have broader appeal than activities requiring particular skills or equipment.

Gender differences are relatively small in activities such as kayaking, but much larger in golf and fishing, suggesting that participation gaps depend strongly on the activity.

02

What Encourages or Prevents Participation?

Explore how motivations and barriers differ across demographic groups.

Health and fitness remain important across age groups, while enjoyment, social interaction and hobbies vary with age. This suggests that encouraging participation may require different approaches for different life stages.

Health-related barriers are more frequently reported by female
non-participants, while physically demanding work is more frequently reported by males. Different barriers may require different approaches to increasing participation.

03

Where Does Recreation Take Place?

Explore regional participation, national park locations and park visitation.

Weekly participation varies across states and territories, showing that national participation figures can conceal regional differences. These differences warrant closer investigation rather than assuming that recreation habits are uniform nationwide.

National parks in the dataset are concentrated along parts of
eastern and coastal Australia, while inland areas show fewer locations. This reveals an uneven geographic distribution of the parks represented in the dataset.

High visitor numbers are concentrated in several parks around the greater Sydney region, suggesting that proximity to major population centres may play an important role in park visitation.

04

How Have Recreation Habits Changed?

A historical comparison of leisure activities among Australian children.

Internet access increased substantially between 2006 and 2012, while bike riding and reading declined modestly. This reflects a changing mix of childhood leisure activities, without establishing that one trend caused another.

HISTORICAL PERSPECTIVE

Changing Childhood Leisure Habits

Internet access increased by 24.8 percentage points between 2006 and 2012.

Over the same period, bike riding declined by 4.3 percentage points, while reading for pleasure declined by 3.4 percentage points.

These trends describe historical changes and do not establish that increased internet access caused reduced participation in other activities.

Conclusion

Recreation participation varies by activity, demographic group and location. Understanding motivations and barriers can help identify opportunities to encourage wider participation.
